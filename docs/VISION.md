# Vision

## One sentence

Peak is a social network that treats attention, data, and relationships as
belonging to the user — funded by people who choose to pay for it, not by advertisers.

## The problem we're reacting to

Facebook and Instagram are extraordinarily well-engineered products aimed at the wrong
objective. When the objective function is *time-on-app* (a proxy for ad inventory),
the rational design choices are:

- **Rank for arousal.** Content that makes you angry or anxious keeps you scrolling, so
  it gets amplified. This is not a bug; it falls out of the incentive.
- **Remove stopping cues.** Infinite scroll, autoplay, no "you're done" marker.
- **Manufacture obligation.** Streaks, "seen" receipts, notification badges engineered
  to itch.
- **Hoard the graph.** If leaving means abandoning your friends and your history, you
  won't leave, so portability is deliberately bad.
- **Keep ranking opaque.** A legible algorithm can be criticized and gamed; an opaque
  one can't be argued with.
- **Squeeze creators.** They bring the audience but don't own it, so terms can move
  against them at any time.

None of this requires the people who work there to be malicious. It requires only that
the company sells ads.

## Our counter-position

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **You are not the product** | Zero ads. Revenue = optional subscription, low creator take-rate, self-host licensing. |
| **You own your feed** | Multiple named feeds; chronological always available; custom rule-based feeds; open-source ranking. |
| **You own your data** | One-click full export in open formats. Real deletion. Minimal retention. |
| **You own your graph** | Federated (ActivityPub). Move instances and keep your followers. Creators can export subscriber lists. |
| **Attention has an off switch** | No infinite scroll by default. "Caught up" markers. Session-time awareness. Batch/digest mode. |
| **Context is respected** | Circles (Close Friends / Family / Work / Public) are a core primitive, not a setting. |
| **Openness is structural** | AGPL-3.0. Open API. Self-hostable. Third-party clients welcomed. |
| **Safety is designed in** | Strong block semantics, stackable community moderation + labels, teen-safe defaults. |
| **Wellbeing over metrics** | Ranking explicitly does *not* optimize engagement. Post-session "was this worth your time?" feedback trains it. |

## What success looks like

- A person can use Peak for a year and feel it was **time well spent**, and say so.
- A creator can make a **living** on it and know the rules won't move against them overnight.
- A user can **leave** — export everything, or move to another instance — in one afternoon,
  and we consider that a feature working as intended.
- The ranking system can be **read, criticized, and forked** by anyone.
- If our company disappeared, the network would **keep running** on community instances.

## Non-goals

- Being the biggest. Being the one people don't regret.
- "Neutrality" as an excuse for amplifying harm. Ranking is a set of choices; we make
  ours explicitly and publish them.
- Growth hacks that depend on manufactured anxiety.
- Crypto tokens, engagement mining, or "web3" mechanics.
